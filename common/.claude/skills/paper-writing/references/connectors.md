# Connectors: choosing a conjunction that adds meaning

A connective is not glue. It states the logical relation between two ideas, so the
right one does real work: it tells the reader *why* sentence *i+1* follows sentence
*i* (because of it, despite it, in addition to it, as a consequence of it). Andrew's
standing rule: **hand-pick every connective to name the actual relation, never drop
one in as filler.** A generic "and", "also", "moreover", or "thus" that could be
deleted without loss is filler; replace it or cut it.

## How to use this file

1. Name the relation between the two ideas first (cause? concession? mere addition?).
2. Pick the connective from that category whose shade of meaning is exactly right.
   The categories below note the shades that are easy to get wrong.
3. Vary your choices. Reusing "however" or "moreover" every paragraph flattens the
   prose. **Diversity is welcome, and this list is a starting point, not a fence;**
   reach for any precise connective, including ones not listed here.
4. Prefer a subordinator that builds the relation into the sentence ("since",
   "because", "whereas", "once", "although") over a bolt-on sentence-initial adverb
   when it reads more tightly. `Since the list is printed, every edge is checkable`
   is tighter than `The list is printed. Therefore, every edge is checkable.`
5. Keep the house rules: present-tense active voice, and no em dash in Andrew's prose
   (a connective often lets you replace a dash cleanly).

A caution on register: heavy Latinate sentence-openers (moreover, furthermore,
consequently, nevertheless, notwithstanding) are correct but wear out fast. Do not
open three sentences in a row with one. Often the tightest fix is a subordinate
clause or a mid-sentence connective, not another opener. The lists below are pruned
to connectives usable in formal paper prose; conversational fillers ("at the end of
the day", "the thing is", "basically") are deliberately left out.

## Addition and accumulation
*Add a further point of the same kind.*

and, also, too, moreover, furthermore, in addition, additionally, in addition to,
besides, further, what is more, as well as, together with, coupled with, along with,
not to mention, equally, equally important, again, then, nor, in the same vein,
beyond that, on top of that, not only ... but also.

- Shade: **moreover / furthermore** add a *stronger or more important* point, not just
  another one; if the next point is merely one more item, plain "and" or "also" is
  honest. **besides** leans slightly dismissive of what precedes ("besides, the cost
  is trivial"). **nor** continues a negative ("the list does not help, nor does
  recoloring").

## Similarity and comparison
*Signal that two things behave alike.*

likewise, similarly, in the same way, in the same manner, in like manner, in similar
fashion, correspondingly, by the same token, just as ... so, in comparison,
comparably, comparatively, analogously, along the same lines, consistent with, in
keeping with, relative to, resembling, equivalently, mirroring.

- Shade: use these only when the parallel is real. **by the same token** implies the
  *same underlying reason* produces both, a stronger claim than "similarly".
  **relative to / in comparison** set up a measured comparison, not a loose analogy.

## Contrast and opposition
*Set two ideas against each other.* This is the family most often misused, so choose
with care.

but, yet, however, whereas, while, in contrast, by contrast, on the other hand, on
the contrary, conversely, instead, rather, then again, at the same time,
alternatively, unlike, different from, in opposition to, contrary to.

- **however / but** general contrast, the default.
- **whereas / while** *parallel* juxtaposition of two co-present facts ("Qwen gains,
  whereas InternVL stalls"); no surprise implied.
- **yet** contrast that is *surprising* given what precedes.
- **conversely** the *reversed* case ("large graphs hurt; conversely, tiny ones
  saturate"); do not use it as a synonym for "however".
- **on the contrary** *corrects* a false statement ("It is not slow; on the contrary,
  it is the fastest arm"); it does not merely add a contrasting fact, so it is wrong
  after a true sentence.
- **instead / rather** replace a rejected option with the correct one.
- **at the same time** softens a contrast the way "that said" does; do not confuse it
  with its literal temporal sense.

## Concession
*Grant a point, then push past it.* Concession admits something true, then limits its
force; it is not the same as plain contrast.

although, though, even though, even if, while, despite, in spite of, nevertheless,
nonetheless, notwithstanding, admittedly, granted (that), of course, to be sure,
still, all the same, that said, regardless (of this), albeit, be that as it may, up
to a point, and yet, for all that, while it is true that, while acknowledging.

- Shade: **nevertheless / nonetheless** carry the argument forward *despite* the
  conceded point ("The gain is small; nonetheless, the direction is telling").
  **admittedly / granted / to be sure / while it is true that** open the concession
  itself, usually paired with a later "but" that reclaims the ground. **albeit**
  concedes inside a phrase ("a gain, albeit a small one"). **notwithstanding** can
  follow its noun ("hardware limits notwithstanding").

## Cause and reason
*Introduce why something holds.*

because, since, as, for, owing to, due to, on account of, given (that), in that,
seeing that, inasmuch as, insofar as, in view of, in light of, by virtue of, as a
result of, on the grounds that, considering (that).

- Shade: **because / since / as** all mark cause, but **since** and **as** also carry a
  time sense and can read as ambiguous, so use **because** when the cause is the whole
  point. **given that / in that / considering that** suit a premise the reader already
  accepts. Reserve **due to** for after a noun ("the drop due to size"); use
  **because** for a clause. **insofar as / inasmuch as** limit the cause to a degree
  or respect.

## Effect, consequence, result
*Introduce what follows from a cause.*

therefore, thus, hence, consequently, accordingly, as a result, as a consequence, so,
then, for this reason, it follows that, which is why, thereby, thereupon, whereupon,
in effect, with the result that, so ... that.

- Shade: **thus / hence** suit a conclusion drawn by reasoning; **as a result /
  consequently / accordingly** suit a real-world outcome; **thereby** links a result
  to the action that produced it in the same sentence ("printing the list, thereby
  freeing the budget"); **in effect** flags a restatement of the net upshot. Do not
  chain two of these in one sentence.

## Condition
*Make one clause depend on another.*

if, unless, provided (that), providing that, as long as, so long as, in case, only if,
even if, on condition that, assuming (that), on the assumption that, in the event
that, should (inversion: "should the chain overrun ..."), given (that), but for.

- Shade: **provided that / as long as / on condition that** state a *required*
  condition; **even if** concedes the condition may not matter to the outcome;
  **but for** means "were it not for". **unless** = "if not", so avoid pairing it with
  a second negative.

## Purpose
*State the aim of an action.*

to, in order to, so as to, so that, in order that, with the aim of, with a view to,
for the purpose of, to the end that, with this intention, with this in mind, lest (to
prevent).

- Shade: prefer bare **to** or **so that** in tight prose; **in order to** is fine but
  heavier. **lest** means "for fear that / to prevent" and takes no "not".

## Exemplification and illustration
*Give an instance.*

for example, for instance, such as, e.g., as an illustration, to illustrate, to
demonstrate, to exemplify, namely, in particular, particularly, including, a case in
point, consider, take ... for example, as exemplified by, to name a few, specifically.

- Shade: **namely / in particular / specifically** point to a *specific* referent
  already implied, not an open-ended example; **e.g.** belongs in parentheses, "for
  example" in prose; **such as** introduces representative members, not an exhaustive
  set.

## Clarification and restatement
*Say the same thing more precisely.*

that is, that is to say, in other words, i.e., to put it differently, to put it
another way, to rephrase, to clarify, more precisely, more exactly, namely, or rather,
meaning that, put simply, in plain terms.

- Shade: use these sparingly. If a sentence needs restating, the first statement was
  probably imprecise; fix it rather than append "in other words". **or rather**
  signals a self-correction, so it replaces the prior wording rather than echoing it.

## Emphasis and intensification
*Mark that a point matters.*

indeed, in fact, as a matter of fact, notably, crucially, importantly, above all, more
to the point, most compellingly, remarkably, strikingly, decisively, tellingly,
markedly, significantly, without question, without doubt, undoubtedly, clearly,
certainly, surely, it should be stressed that, it is worth noting that, of particular
note.

- Shade: **indeed / in fact / as a matter of fact** confirm and strengthen the prior
  claim; do not use them to introduce a *new* claim. Earn "crucially", "strikingly",
  and "decisively"; if everything is crucial, nothing is. In ML prose, reserve
  "significantly" for statistical significance to avoid ambiguity.

## Salience and ranking
*Order points by importance.*

primarily, chiefly, principally, mainly, above all, first and foremost, most
importantly, of particular importance, of particular note, secondarily, of less
importance, least of all, first of all.

## Logical sequence and enumeration
*Order steps in an argument (not in time).*

first, second, third, next, then, finally, lastly, to begin with, in the first place,
in the first instance, initially, subsequently, in turn, having established that, from
here, at this point, thereafter, following this.

- Shade: keep enumeration in prose, not as a colon-list (Andrew's rule). "We first
  ask ...; we then ...; finally we ..." reads better than "three steps: a, b, c".

## Time and chronology
*Order events that actually happened in time (procedures, training runs).*

before, after, once, when, while, as, during, until, till, meanwhile, in the meantime,
subsequently, afterwards, then, at that point, by the time, prior to, following,
simultaneously, concurrently, eventually, soon, shortly, immediately, as soon as, now
that, up to now, until now, formerly, previously, later, at length.

## Place and space
*Rare in ML prose, but useful for figures and layouts.*

above, below, adjacent to, opposite to, alongside, next to, near, nearby, beyond, in
the foreground, in the background, on the left/right, in the center of, throughout,
where, wherever, amid, among, between, across.

## Reference and topic shift
*Move the reader to a new subject or scope a statement to one.*

regarding, with regard to, with respect to, as regards, concerning, as for, in terms
of, turning to, as to, on the subject of, in the case of, when it comes to.

- Shade: **with respect to / in terms of** narrow a claim to one dimension ("in terms
  of paired accuracy"); do not overuse "in terms of" as a catch-all preposition.

## Summary and conclusion
*Close a passage.*

in summary, in short, in brief, to sum up, to summarize, in sum, overall, on the
whole, taken together, all told, all in all, all things considered, in the final
analysis, given these points, in essence, in a word, on balance, by and large, in
conclusion, to conclude, ultimately, in the end.

- Shade: prefer **taken together / overall / on balance** mid-paper; save **in
  conclusion / to conclude** for the actual conclusion.

## Generalization and qualification
*Scope a claim.*

in general, generally, broadly, as a rule, for the most part, typically, on balance,
to a first approximation, in most cases, with few exceptions, strictly speaking, by
and large, ordinarily, in principle.
